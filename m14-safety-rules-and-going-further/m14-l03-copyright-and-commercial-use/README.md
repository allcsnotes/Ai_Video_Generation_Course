# M14-L03 · Copyright and Commercial Use

> Video: Module 14, Lesson 3. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

This ad sells chai for a shop. That makes it **commercial use**: it is made to earn money. So before you make videos like this for clients, you need to know two things: who owns your AI video, and when you are allowed to sell it.

## What you'll learn

- Who owns an AI video: what the tools say, and what copyright law says
- What you add yourself, and why it matters
- When you can sell your video: free plan vs paid membership on Kling, and Flow's terms
- Why the watermark stays on a free plan
- Music, logos and brands: the big risks in paid work
- Five checks before you deliver paid work

## Key ideas

### Ownership: what the tools say

- **Kling's terms** say: "we do not claim ownership of the content".
- **Google** says the same for **Flow**.

### Ownership: what copyright law says

**Copyright** is the legal right to say who may copy and use a creative work. It is a different question from what the tool says.

- In the United States, the **Copyright Office** says a **prompt alone does not make you the author**.
- What you add yourself can be protected: your script, your voice, your edit, and how you choose and arrange the clips.

So do real creative work on top of the AI clips. This is not legal advice, and the rules differ in each country.

### Can you sell it? It depends on your plan

| Kling terms | What they say |
|---|---|
| Main terms | Without written permission, you may not use the output for any commercial purpose |
| Paid terms (for paying members) | Commercial use is not restricted, and the watermark can be removed |

- **Buying credits is not the same as a membership.** Check your plan before you make client work.
- **For Flow**, Google points you to its Terms of Service, and does not claim ownership. Read those terms for your plan too.

### On a free plan, the watermark stays

- Kling's terms say outputs must carry the Kling brand, unless you have permission.
- So **never crop it, blur it, or cover it**. That breaks the terms.
- If a client wants a clean video, **upgrade** to a plan that removes the watermark. That is the only safe way.

### Music: licensed only

Music is the next big risk.

- **Never use film songs or famous tracks.** YouTube will find them, and the owner can claim your video, or take it down.
- **Use music with a clear licence.** A **licence** is written permission that says how you may use something. The video shows Pixabay's Content License Summary: you can use its music for free, without credit, and you can edit it into new work.
- But you **cannot sell the track on its own**. And content with a famous logo in it cannot be used to sell goods.
- **Save the licence page and the track name** for every song you use.

### Logos and brands

Never put any of these into an AI video you sell:

- a real brand's logo
- a famous cartoon character
- a celebrity

That is why our shop is called **Chai Corner**. We invented it, so nobody else owns that name or that sign. Your client's own logo is fine, but only with their **written permission**.

## Worked example

Before you deliver paid work, the video runs five checks. Here they are for a client ad like our chai ad:

| # | Check | For the chai ad |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does your plan allow commercial use? | Paid membership, not just bought credits |
| 2 | Do you have a licence for the music? | A licensed track, licence page and track name saved |
| 3 | No real brands, and no real people without consent | An invented brand, or the client's own logo with written permission |
| 4 | The AI label is on | Yes, it looks real |
| 5 | A record of your prompts, licences, and permissions | Saved in the project folder |

All five must be a yes before you send the file.

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking bought credits allow selling.** On Kling, credits are not a membership.
- **Removing the watermark yourself.** Never crop, blur or cover it. Upgrade instead.
- **Using a popular song "just for a few seconds".** The owner can still claim or remove your video.
- **Adding a famous logo or cartoon character to a client ad.** Only invented brands, or the client's own logo with written permission.
- **Keeping no records.** Save the prompts, licences and permissions for every paid job.

## Answers
