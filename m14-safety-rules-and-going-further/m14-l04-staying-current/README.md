# M14-L04 · Staying Current

> Video: Module 14, Lesson 4. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

A few months ago, Kling 3.0 was the newest model. Today, the model list shows Kling 4.0, with a New badge. AI video tools change every few months. This lesson shows how new models appear, and how to test a new tool in ten minutes.

## What you'll learn

- How to read a tool's release notes, version numbers and model names
- What to do when an old model is leaving
- Where to look for real news, and why to ignore the hype
- A ten minute test for any new tool or model, with the same three prompts every time
- How to score the results and decide: switch, keep both, or skip

## Key ideas

### How new models appear

Every tool keeps a page of **release notes**: a list of updates, each with a date and what changed. The video shows Kling's:

| Date | What changed |
|---|---|
| 15 October 2026 | Kling 4.0 arrived, marked as a major release |
| June 2026 | Kling 3.0 Turbo came out |

### Read the version number

| Change | Example | Means |
|---|---|---|
| A new first number | 3.0 > 4.0 | a big new model |
| A small step | 1.1 > 1.2 | an update to the same model |

The names help too:

| Name | Usually means |
|---|---|
| Flash, Turbo, Lite | faster and cheaper |
| Quality | slower, better, and more credits |

Inside the app, a new model appears **at the top of the list**, with a **New** badge.

### Old models also leave

In Flow, three video models are marked **Leaving Dec 5**. So never build your work around one model. Keep the parts you own:

- your script
- your prompts
- your start frames
- your character images

When a model leaves, run the same prompts on the new one.

### Where to look for news

1. The tool's own **release notes** page
2. The **model list** inside the app (look for the New badge)
3. The tool's **official blog or help pages**: trust the real page, not a rumour

And ignore the hype. A demo video shows the best clip out of many tries. Your own test is the only proof.

## Worked example: the ten minute test

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 and 2 | Check the basics: the price of a clip, the longest length, the resolution, and the commercial use terms |
| 3 to 7 | Run your test kit: the same three prompts every time, on the cheapest setting |
| 8 to 10 | Put the new clips next to your old ones, and compare |

### The test kit

The video's three test prompts, each testing something different:

```text
1 · Person: our character Meera pours chai into a glass.
    Tests the face and the hands.

2 · Motion: rain falls from a tin roof.
    Tests water, light, and movement.

3 · Product: a jar of Chai Corner tea on a table.
    Tests the label text.
```

Write your own full prompts from these ideas (with the prompt formula from Module 3), save them, and never change them between tests. Then every model faces the same test.

### Score each clip from 1 to 5

Score the face, the motion, and how well it follows your prompt. Then work out the cost per usable second, like in Module 8 (all the credits you spent, divided by the seconds you kept). The video's example scores:

| Example scores | Old model | New model |
|---|---|---|
| Face | 3 | 4 |
| Motion | 4 | 4 |
| Follows the prompt | 3 | 5 |
| Cost per usable second | 4 credits | 3 credits |

### Then decide

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| The new model wins clearly | Switch |
| It is close | Keep using both |
| It loses | Skip it, and test again next month |

In the example above, the new model wins clearly, so you switch. Write the scores in one simple sheet, so every new model faces the same test. The [cost-per-usable-second sheet](../../downloads/cost-per-usable-second.csv) from Module 8 works for the cost column.

## Common mistakes

- **Believing a demo video.** It shows the best clip out of many tries. Run your own test.
- **Testing with a new prompt each time.** Use the same three prompts, or the scores mean nothing.
- **Testing on the most expensive setting.** Use the cheapest setting for the test.
- **Building everything around one model.** Models leave. Keep your scripts, prompts, start frames and character images.
- **Trusting a rumour.** Check the release notes, the model list, or the official help pages.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: save your three test prompts in a note, and run them on the newest model in one tool.
2. Open the release notes page of one tool you use. Write down the newest date and what changed.
3. Score your three clips for face, motion and follows the prompt, and decide: switch, keep both, or skip.
