# M14-L02 · Labelling AI Content

> Video: Module 14, Lesson 2. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Here is a short clip of rain at a chai stall. It looks real, but every frame was made by AI (Kling 4.0, from a text prompt). So, before you post it, the platforms want you to tell viewers. This lesson covers when you must add a label, how to add it on YouTube, Instagram and Facebook, and when you can skip it.

## What you'll learn

- What an AI label is, and who it protects
- YouTube's four cases where you must disclose AI, and what does not need a label
- How to add the label in YouTube Studio (Attributes > AI use > Yes)
- Meta's label for Instagram and Facebook ("AI info"), and when it is needed
- How to decide for each of our builds
- Two more habits: keep the watermark, and say it in words

## Key ideas

### What an AI label is

A **label** is a small note that says: this was made or changed with AI. Viewers see it on the video, or under it.

- It **protects your viewers**, because they know what is real.
- It **protects you**, because hiding AI can get your video removed.
- The good news: on YouTube, a label alone does **not** lower your views, or what you can earn.

### YouTube: when you must disclose

The video shows the YouTube help page on AI content ("Disclosing GenAI content"). YouTube says you must disclose AI content that **looks real**. That means four things:

1. A real person saying or doing something they did not
2. A real event or place that was changed
3. A realistic scene that never happened
4. AI music that is the main focus

You do **not** need a label for:

- things that are clearly not real, like a unicorn in a fantasy world
- small edits, like colour or captions
- a script written with AI

### YouTube: how to add it

Upload in **YouTube Studio**. In the **Attributes** section, under **AI use**, choose **Yes**. That is all.

```text
YouTube Studio > Upload > Attributes > AI use > Yes
```

YouTube can also add a label by itself, when it finds AI signals in the file. And if you keep hiding AI, it can remove videos, or stop your earnings.

### Instagram and Facebook: "AI info"

Instagram and Facebook belong to **Meta**, and Meta calls its label **AI info**.

| Meta asks for a label on | No label needed |
|---|---|
| Video that looks real | Pictures |
| Realistic sound, like an AI voiceover in a reel | A cartoon style landscape |
| Meta's example: a realistic video of people at a market | |

To add it, look for the **AI label option** in the settings, just before you share. Meta may also add it by itself, and there can be penalties if a needed label is missing.

### When in doubt, label it

When you are not sure, add the label. It costs nothing, and it keeps you safe.

### Two more habits

- **Keep the tool watermark**, and never try to remove a label. The watermark tells viewers which AI tool made it. In India, the new rules say nobody may remove a label.
- **Say it in words too.** Add a line like "Made with AI" to your caption or description.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the label because the clip is short.** Length does not matter: real-looking means label it.
- **Labelling only in the caption.** Use the platform's own setting (AI use on YouTube, the AI label option on Meta), and add the caption line as well.
- **Cropping or covering the watermark.** Keep it. Never remove a label.
- **Labelling small edits.** Colour, captions, or an AI-written script do not need a label on YouTube.
