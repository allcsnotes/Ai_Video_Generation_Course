# M14-L01 · Deepfakes and Consent

> Video: Module 14, Lesson 1. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Meera and Ravi starred in our short film. Neither of them is a real person. We made both of them from text, and that was on purpose. This lesson is about why: what is never okay, and how to stay safe when a real person is involved.

## What you'll learn

- What a deepfake is, and why it is so easy to make one today
- Five things that are never okay, not even as a joke
- How to get written consent when a real person is part of the job (four things the form must say)
- What the AI tools' own rules ban (the Google policy for Flow and AI Studio)
- What the law in India says since February 2026
- The safe way to work, and three questions to ask before you post

## Key ideas

### What a deepfake is

A **deepfake** is a video, a photo, or a voice of a real person, made or changed by AI, so they seem to say or do something they never did.

| A real person | AI | The fake |
|---|---|---|
| A few photos, a few seconds of voice | makes or changes a video, photo, or voice | they seem to say or do something they never did |

Today this takes only a few photos and a few seconds of someone's voice. That is why the rules matter so much.

**Meera is not a deepfake.** She was made from words, and she looks like no real person. The problem starts when AI copies a **real face** or a **real voice**.

### Never okay, not even as a joke

1. **Intimate fakes.** Never make sexual or intimate pictures of a real person. And never make anything like that with a child.
2. **Voice scams.** Never clone a voice to trick someone, like a fake call from a family member asking for money.
3. **Fake news.** Never make a politician or a famous person say something they did not say, and never fake a news event.
4. **Fake endorsements.** Never use a celebrity's face to sell a product. An **endorsement** is a famous person saying they back a product; faking one is wrong even if the ad looks great.
5. **Mocking real people.** Never make fun of a friend, a classmate, or a colleague with a fake video. It can hurt them for years.

### With a real person: written consent

Sometimes a real person really is part of the job, like a shop owner who wants to appear in their own ad. Then you need their **consent** (their clear permission), **in writing**. A simple form they sign is enough to start.

The form should say four things:

1. What you will make
2. Where it will be shown
3. For how long
4. That they can say stop at any time

Keep a copy. And use their face and voice only for that **one job**. Permission for one video is not permission for every video.

### What the tools say

The AI tools have rules too. The video shows the Google policy for its AI tools, like Flow and AI Studio (Google's Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy). It bans:

- intimate images made without consent
- using someone's personal data without the consent the law needs
- pretending to be a real person, living or dead, to fool people
- claiming AI work was made only by a human

Other tools have similar rules. Break them, and you can lose your account, and all your credits.

### The law in India

- In **February 2026**, India updated its **IT Rules** for AI content.
- AI content that looks real must carry a **clear label**, and nobody is allowed to remove that label.
- Big platforms must ask if your upload was made with AI, and must take down harmful deepfakes **within hours**.
- Faking a real person can also break older laws: on cheating, privacy, and damaging someone's name.

This is not legal advice. Check the rules where you live.

### The safe way to work

This is how the whole course works:

- Make your characters **from text**, like Meera and Ravi.
- Show **your own products**, or a client's, with their permission.

Before you post, ask three questions:

1. Is this a real person?
2. Do I have their written consent?
3. Could anyone be fooled, or hurt?

If any answer worries you, do not post it.

## Worked example

A shop owner wants to appear in their own Chai Corner style ad. Here is how the lesson's steps apply:

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| Is a real person involved? | Yes, the shop owner |
| Consent | A simple written form they sign |
| What the form says | what you will make, where it will be shown, for how long, and that they can say stop at any time |
| After signing | Keep a copy; use their face and voice only for this one ad |
| Before posting | Ask the three questions; label it if it looks real |

If the client later asks for a second, different video with their face, that needs new permission. Permission for one video is not permission for every video.

## Common mistakes

- **"It is only a joke."** A fake video of a friend or colleague can hurt them for years. It is never okay.
- **Verbal permission only.** Get it in writing, and keep a copy.
- **Reusing consent.** One signed form covers one job, not every future video.
- **Thinking a made-up character is risky.** Meera, made from words, is not a deepfake. Copying a real face or voice is the problem.
- **Ignoring the tool's rules.** Breaking them can cost you your account and all your credits.
