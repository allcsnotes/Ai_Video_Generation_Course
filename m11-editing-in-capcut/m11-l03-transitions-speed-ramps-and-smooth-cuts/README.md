# M11-L03 · Transitions, Speed Ramps and Smooth Cuts

> Video: Module 11, Lesson 3. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Every cut joins two shots. A **plain cut** just jumps from one shot to the next. The video shows a plain cut, then the same two shots with a smooth cut. This lesson teaches three smooth tools that are already built into CapCut, with no downloads.

## What you'll learn

- Why you do not need CapCut's Transitions tab for smooth cuts
- Cut on action: hide the jump between two AI shots
- Speed ramps: Speed > Curve presets, and why they change the length
- A fade to black with two opacity keyframes
- Which tool to use when, and why less is more

## Before you start

- CapCut on the computer, with the project from the last lesson (wide, pour, close-up, the voiceover and the music).
- Nothing in this lesson spends credits.
- CapCut has a **Transitions** tab full of ready-made effects. It downloads them from the internet, and on some computers the list does not load. The good news: the smoothest cuts do not need it. Three tools are already built in:

| Tool | In one line |
|---|---|
| Cut on action | Cut during a move |
| Speed ramp | Fast, then normal |
| Fade | To or from black |

## Follow along

### Tool 1 · Cut on action

Cut while something is moving, and let the next shot carry on the same move.

| Cut | What you see |
|---|---|
| Jumpy | The wide shot ends with her smiling at the camera. Then suddenly, she is pouring. It jumps |
| On action | Cut while her hand reaches for the kettle. The next shot starts as she lifts it. The eye follows the hand, and hardly sees the cut |

How: you only move your **Split** points, just like in the last lesson.

### Tool 2 · A speed ramp

A **speed ramp** changes the clip's speed: fast, then back to normal.

1. Select a clip (the video uses the pour).
2. Click path: `Speed > Curve`.
3. Pick a ready-made curve. CapCut has presets like **Montage**, **Hero time**, and **Flash in**.
4. The video picks **Flash in**: it starts fast, then settles to normal speed.

Reading the graph: high means fast, and the middle line is normal speed.

A faster clip is also a **shorter** clip. Our pour dropped from about 2 seconds to 1.

### Tool 3 · A fade out with keyframes

Fade the last shot to black, so the video ends softly.

1. Select the close-up. Click path: `Video > Blend > Opacity`.
2. Put the playhead **1 second before the end**, and click the **diamond**. That is a **keyframe**: it saves the opacity, 100%, at that moment.
3. Move to the last frame, and drag **Opacity** down to **0**. CapCut adds a second keyframe by itself.
4. Between the two diamonds, the picture fades out. Halfway, it is about 50%.

### Step 4 · Fix the length, fade the music

The speed ramp made the ad shorter than the voice. So the video set the pour back to **None** (normal speed). Then:

Click path: select the music > `Fade out` > **1 s**.

### Which one, and when?

| Tool | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Plain cut | Most of the time. Quick and clean |
| Cut on action | Hiding the jump between two AI shots |
| Speed ramp | Adding energy |
| Fade | The start, the end, or a jump in time |

Keep it simple: one or two tricks in an ad is plenty.

## Prompts used

No AI prompts in this lesson. Settings used:

```text
Speed > Curve > Flash in            (tried, then set back to None)
Video > Blend > Opacity: keyframe 100% at 1 s before the end, 0% on the last frame
Music > Fade out: 1 s
```

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: make one cut on action, and fade out the end of your ad, the picture and the music.
2. Try Flash in on one clip and note how much shorter it gets. Does the ad still cover the voiceover?

What good looks like: the cut lands mid-move so the eye follows the action; the last second fades from 100% to 0%; the music fades out over 1 second; the ad is still as long as the voice.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting for the Transitions list to load.** You do not need it; cut on action and fades are built in.
- **Cutting when nothing moves.** The jump shows. Cut during a move.
- **Forgetting a speed ramp shortens the clip.** The ad can end before the voice.
- **Fading only the picture.** Fade the music too.
- **Too many tricks.** One or two in an ad is plenty; plain cuts most of the time.
