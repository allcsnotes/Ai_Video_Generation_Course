# M11-L06 · Export Settings and Quality Checks

> Video: Module 11, Lesson 6. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Our shot is edited. Now it has to leave the editor as **one video file**, ready for Instagram, YouTube and the rest. This lesson sets the export and checks the file before you upload.

**Note:** CapCut asks you to sign in before it exports, and that sign in does not work on every computer. So this lesson exports from **Shotcut** again (the same project as Lesson 5). Shotcut saves files fully offline. The settings and checks are the same in any editor.

Printable checklist: [export checklist](../../downloads/export-checklist.md).

## What you'll learn

- The right shape for Reels, Shorts, TikTok and YouTube
- The one format every app accepts: MP4, H.264, 30 fps
- Shotcut's export settings: the YouTube preset, Quality and Audio
- How to check the real file after export
- What to look for before you upload, and why you watch it on your phone

## Before you start

- **Shotcut** (free, offline), with your edited project from Lesson 5.
- Nothing in this lesson spends credits.

### Pick the shape

| Shape | Size | For |
|---|---|---|
| Tall · 9:16 | 1080 × 1920 | Reels, Shorts, TikTok |
| Wide · 16:9 | 1920 × 1080 | A normal YouTube video |

You set the shape in the project's **Video mode**, before you edit.

### The format every app accepts

| Setting | What it is |
|---|---|
| MP4 | The file type |
| H.264 | The video codec (the way the picture is packed into the file) |
| 30 fps | Frames per second |

## Follow along

### Step 1 · Pick the YouTube preset

Click **Export**. In the list of presets, pick **YouTube**. It sets MP4 and H.264 for you, and it works for Reels and Shorts too.

### Step 2 · Check the Video tab

Under **Video**, check the **Resolution**, 1080 × 1920, and the frame rate, **30**. Both come from the project.

### Step 3 · Raise the Quality

Open the **Codec** tab. **Quality** decides how sharp the file is. Raise it to **70%**.

The file gets bigger, but every app squeezes your video again when you upload, so start with a sharp one.

### Step 4 · Audio

In the **Audio** tab, keep **AAC** at **384** kilobits per second (384k). That is plenty for voice and music.

### Step 5 · Export

Click **Export Video/Audio**, name the file, and save it.

What you should see: the **Jobs** panel shows the progress, and a **green tick** when it is done.

### Step 6 · Check the real file

Right click the job, and click **Open**. The **Properties** panel shows the real file: 1080 × 1920, at 30 frames per second.

### Step 7 · Watch it before you upload

Watch the whole file, from start to end. Look for:

- blurry or blocky patches
- captions cut off at the edges
- sound that is too quiet or too loud

### Step 8 · Watch it on your phone

Last, send it to your phone, and watch it there, full screen. That is how your viewers will see it.

## Prompts used

No AI prompts in this lesson. Export settings used:

```text
Export > Presets: YouTube (MP4, H.264)
Video: Resolution 1080 × 1920, 30 fps (from the project)
Codec: Quality 70%
Audio: AAC, 384k
Export Video/Audio > name > Save
Jobs > right click > Open > Properties: 1080 × 1920, 30 fps
```

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: export your Chai Corner ad, and watch it on your phone before you post it.
2. Open your exported file's Properties and write down its resolution and frame rate. Do they match your project?
3. Go through the [export checklist](../../downloads/export-checklist.md) for your file.

What good looks like: a 1080 × 1920, 30 fps MP4 that looks sharp, with every caption inside the frame and clear sound, checked on a phone at full screen.

## Common mistakes

- **Wrong shape.** Tall 9:16 for Reels and Shorts; wide 16:9 for normal YouTube. Set it in Video mode before you edit.
- **Low quality to save space.** Apps squeeze every upload again; start sharp.
- **Trusting the settings without checking the file.** Open it and read the Properties.
- **Only checking on a big screen.** Most viewers watch on a phone, full screen.
- **Not watching to the end.** Blocky patches, cut-off captions and sound problems can be anywhere.
