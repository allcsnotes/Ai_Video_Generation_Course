# M11-L01 · A Tour of CapCut

> Video: Module 11, Lesson 1. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Three shots, a voiceover, and a music track. Right now they are just five separate files. An **editor** joins them into one ad. This lesson tours CapCut on the computer and lays the five files on the timeline.

## What you'll learn

- How to start a new project in CapCut
- The four areas of the editor: media, player, details, timeline
- How to import your files, and why you number them
- How to build the ad on the timeline, one track per sound
- Why you set the project to 1080 × 1920 before anything else

## Before you start

- **CapCut on the computer** (the desktop app). The video says it is free, and you can start editing without signing in. Some CapCut features need its online servers and may not work on every computer; later lessons in this module show offline ways.
- Nothing in this lesson spends credits.
- Your files for one ad, in **one folder** (the video's folder is `Chai Corner ad`), numbered in order:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `1_pour.mp4` | Shot 1 |
| `2_closeup.mp4` | Shot 2 |
| `3_wide.mp4` | Shot 3 |
| `voiceover.wav` | The voice (Module 10, Lesson 2) |
| `music.mp3` | The music (Module 10, Lesson 3) |

## Follow along

### Step 1 · Start a new project

Open CapCut. You land on the **home screen**. Click **New project**.

What you should see: the editor opens straight away. Your project saves by itself as you work: look for **auto saved** at the top.

### Step 2 · Learn the four areas

| Area | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Media panel | Top left | Your files, plus tabs for audio, text, effects, transitions and filters |
| Player | Middle | Shows the frame under the **playhead** (the line that marks the current moment) |
| Details panel | Right | Shows the settings of whatever you select |
| Timeline | Along the bottom | Where you build the video, from left to right |

### Step 3 · Import your files

Click **Import**, and pick your files from your computer.

What you should see: they sit in the media panel. Videos show little pictures. Audio shows a wave.

Tip: keep every file for one ad in one folder, and number the shots in order: one pour, two close-up, three wide.

### Step 4 · Lay the shots on the timeline

1. Drag the first shot down onto the timeline.
2. Drag the next two after it.

What you should see: they snap together, end to end, on the **main track**. Three 5 second shots make 15 seconds (`3 × 5 s = 15 s`).

### Step 5 · Add the voice and the music

1. Drag the voiceover under the shots.
2. Drag the music under that.

Each sound gets its own track. The music was 3:00 long, so the video cut it at 0:15 and deleted the rest. The next lesson covers cutting in detail.

### Step 6 · Look at a clip's settings

Click any clip. The details panel on the right switches to its settings: size, position, and more.

### Step 7 · Set the project first

Do this early. Look at the details panel:

- **Ratio**, **Resolution**, **Frame rate** are listed.
- Resolution says **Adapted**. That means it follows your clips. Ours are only 360 × 640 pixels, so the whole ad would come out small.

### Step 8 · Change the project settings

1. Click **Modify**.
2. Under **Ratio**, pick a shape: 16:9 for YouTube, or 9:16 for Reels.
3. For **Resolution**, choose **Customized**, and type **1080 × 1920**. That is full HD, standing up.
4. Keep the **Frame rate** at **30**, and click **Save**.

What you should see: the details panel now shows 1080 × 1920.

## Prompts used

No AI prompts in this lesson. Settings used:

```text
Ratio: 9:16
Resolution: Customized, 1080 × 1920
Frame rate: 30
```

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: make a project, import three of your clips, a voice, and some music, and lay them on the timeline.
2. Set the project to 1080 × 1920 at 30 frames per second, and check the details panel shows it.

What good looks like: three shots end to end on the main track, the voice on its own track below, the music on another track cut to the length of the shots, and the project at 1080 × 1920.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving Resolution on Adapted.** With 360 × 640 AI clips, the whole ad comes out small. Set 1080 × 1920 first.
- **Files spread across folders, with random names.** Keep one folder per ad and number the shots.
- **Putting the voice and music on the same track.** Each sound gets its own track.
- **Leaving the 3 minute music running.** Cut it to the length of the ad and delete the rest.
