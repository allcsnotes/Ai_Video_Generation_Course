# M15-L06 · Upgrading Your Tools

> Video: Module 15, Lesson 6. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

So far, we used free credits and one small plan. When should you pay for more? Only when the work pays for it. This lesson shows which plan to buy first, and when it pays for itself.

## What you'll learn

- How to make free credits go a long way
- When to upgrade: when a real client is ready to pay you
- Which plan to buy first, and why it matters for selling your videos
- The simple maths of when a plan pays for itself
- Three rules before you upgrade

## Key ideas

### Start free

- Use the **free credits** to learn and to practise.
- **Plan every clip** before you generate, then generate once.
- **Test at a low resolution first** (like 360p), then make the final.
- Free credits go a long way.

**Upgrade when a real client is ready to pay you, and before you make their video.**

### Which plan first?

Buy the tool you use **the most** for paid work.

| Plan | What the video shows |
|---|---|
| Kling Standard | 6.99 US dollars for the first month, with 660 credits a month (when the page was captured) |
| Google AI Plus | the cheapest Google plan; gives you Flow credits every month, for talking characters with sound |

For most beginners, the first plan is a **Kling Standard** plan. It also matters for selling:

- On the **free plan**, Kling does not allow commercial use without permission.
- **Paid members** can use their videos for business (Kling's Terms of Paid Service, from M14-L03).

In Module 7, Google AI Plus was ₹399 a month. Prices and offers change, so check the current price before you buy.

### When does a plan pay for itself?

Do the simple maths. Compare two numbers:

| The plan, for one month | One paid video |
|---|---|
| what you pay | what one client pays you |

- If one paid video earns **more** than the plan costs, the plan **pays for itself after the first client**.
- **No paid work yet? Then do not upgrade.** Use the free credits, and keep practising.

### Three rules before you upgrade

1. **Pay monthly, not yearly**, until the work is steady.
2. **Watch the first month offer.** The price goes up after that. (Kling Standard shows an offer for the first month, then the normal price.)
3. **Cancel what you do not use.** Check your credits at the end of every month.

### Before you pay: a quick check

- [ ] A real client is ready to pay me, and I have not made their video yet
- [ ] This is the tool I use most for paid work
- [ ] The plan allows commercial use (selling the video)
- [ ] One paid video earns more than one month of the plan
- [ ] I am paying monthly, not yearly
- [ ] I wrote down the first month price and the price after it
- [ ] I set a reminder to check my credits at the end of the month

## Worked example

You have one client ready to pay for a 15 second reel, and your floor price from M15-L04 is ₹1,400 (example numbers).

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is a client ready to pay? | Yes, so upgrade now, before you make their video |
| Which tool do you use most? | Kling, so a Kling Standard plan |
| Does it allow selling? | Yes: paid members can use their videos for business |
| Does it pay for itself? | Compare the plan's monthly price (converted to rupees) with the ₹1,400 the client pays. If the video earns more, it pays for itself after this first client |
| Monthly or yearly? | Monthly, until the work is steady |
| After the first month? | Check the normal price, and your unused credits |

## Common mistakes

- **Upgrading before you have paid work.** Practise on free credits first.
- **Buying a yearly plan to save a little.** Pay monthly until the work is steady.
- **Forgetting the first month offer ends.** Note the normal price before you buy.
- **Selling client work from a free plan.** Kling's free plan does not allow commercial use without permission.
- **Paying for tools you do not use.** Check your credits every month, and cancel.
- **Generating at full resolution while testing.** Test low, then make the final.
