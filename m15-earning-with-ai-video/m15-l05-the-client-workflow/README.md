# M15-L05 · The Client Workflow

> Video: Module 15, Lesson 5. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

A client says yes. Now what? A clear **workflow** (the same steps, in the same order, for every job) keeps the client happy, and stops the job from going wrong. This lesson shows five steps, from the first brief to the final file.

## What you'll learn

- Step 1 · The brief: the questions to ask before you make anything
- Step 2 · Script approval: before you spend a single credit
- Step 3 · The first cut: a rough draft, and all changes in one message
- Step 4 · Revisions: counting the rounds from your price
- Step 5 · Delivery: payment first, then the full quality files

## Key ideas

### Step 1 · The brief

A **brief** is a short description of the job. Before you make anything, ask the client a few simple questions:

| Question | Examples |
|---|---|
| What is the video for? | a launch, a sale, an event |
| Where will it be posted? | Instagram, YouTube, a shop screen |
| How long is it, and what shape? | like 15 seconds, 9:16 |
| What must it show? | the product, the logo, the price, or the place |

Write the answers in one short message, and send it back. Now you both agree on the job.

### Step 2 · Script approval

- Write the voiceover and a simple shot list, like we did in Module 3.
- Send it to the client **before you spend a single credit**.
- A change to the script costs nothing. A change after the clips are made costs you credits and time.

### Step 3 · The first cut

- Make the clips, edit them, and send a rough version.
- Send a **small, low quality preview** (the video shows a 480p, small file), and say clearly that it is a **draft**, so they do not expect the final look.
- Ask for **all the changes in one message**, not one at a time.

### Step 4 · Revisions

This is where the two rounds from your price (M15-L04) come in.

| Round | Status |
|---|---|
| Round 1 | Included, part of your price |
| Round 2 | Included, part of your price |
| Round 3 | Costs extra: remind them, kindly |

Fix what they asked for, send the new version, and count that as one round.

### Step 5 · Delivery

1. First, ask for the **rest of the payment**.
2. Then export the final video in **full quality**, in **every size you agreed on**.
3. Share it in a **Google Drive folder**, and keep your own copy of the project.

The video shows the export in Shotcut (Module 11) and Drive's "Share with a link" (M15-L02).

## Worked example: the cardamom cake reel

The video follows one small job for a cafe.

### The brief, written back

```text
For: the new cardamom cake
Where: Instagram
Size: 15 seconds, 9:16
Show: the cake, the logo, the price
```

You both agree on the job.

### The script and shot list

| Shot | What we see | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Shot 1 | Cake comes out of the oven | 5 s |
| Shot 2 | Close up of a slice | 5 s |
| Shot 3 | The counter, with the logo and price | 5 s |

Approved, before you spend a single credit.

### Then

A draft preview at 480p, all changes in one message, two included rounds, the rest of the payment, and the full quality file in a shared Drive folder.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting without a brief.** Ask the questions, and write the answers back.
- **Generating clips before the script is approved.** Every change after that costs credits and time.
- **Sending the first cut in full quality.** Send a small preview, clearly marked as a draft.
- **Taking changes one at a time.** Ask for all of them in one message.
- **Doing a free third round.** Remind the client, kindly, that it costs extra.
- **Delivering before the final payment,** or not keeping your own copy of the project.
