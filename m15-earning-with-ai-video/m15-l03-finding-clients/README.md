# M15-L03 · Finding Clients

> Video: Module 15, Lesson 3. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

You have a portfolio. Now you need someone to pay you. Clients will not find you on their own, so you go to where they are. This lesson covers three places to look, and a first message that gets replies.

## What you'll learn

- How freelance sites work: a gig on Fiverr, a profile and proposals on Upwork
- How to use Instagram as a live portfolio
- How to approach local shops with a sample
- Why most first messages get no reply, and the four parts of a good one
- How to follow up once, and keep a simple sheet

## Key ideas

### First: freelance sites

On **Fiverr** and **Upwork**, people come to hire.

| Site | How it works |
|---|---|
| Fiverr | You create a **gig**: one clear service, like a 15 second product reel, with a price. Buyers come to you. |
| Upwork | You make a free **profile**, then send **proposals** (a short pitch for one job) to jobs that clients post. |

These sites keep a share of every payment, so **check the fee page** before you set your price. Fees change, so read the current page on each site.

### Second: Instagram

- Post your best reels on your own page, so it works like a **live portfolio**.
- Then find small brands with weak or old videos, and send them a short, friendly message, about them, not about you.

### Third: local shops in your area

- Walk in, or message cafes, salons, and gyms.
- Show the owner a sample on your phone. It takes ten seconds.
- Even better, make a **free ten second sample** of their own product. But **never post it without their permission**.

### Why most messages get no reply

Most messages get no reply, because they are **long**, **copied**, and **all about you**. The video shows a bad first message like this one:

```text
Hello sir or madam, I am a professional AI video creator with many skills. I offer video editing, AI reels, ads, explainers, animations, logos, and much more. I have lots of experience and my prices are very low. Please check my profile and contact me if you need anything. Thank you and have a nice day.
```

### A good first message: four parts

A good first message has four parts, and it fits on one phone screen.

1. **Their name**, and something real about their business
2. **One simple idea** for them, not for you
3. **A link to a short sample**
4. **One easy question**, like "Can I make you a free sample?"

## Worked example: a message for a cafe

```text
Hi Priya, I loved the photos of your new cardamom cake. I make short videos for cafes.
A 10 second reel of the cake coming out of the oven could stop people scrolling.
Here is a reel I made for a chai shop: [link to your sample]
Can I make a free sample for you?
```

| Part | In the message |
|---|---|
| 1 · Their name, something real | "Hi Priya, I loved the photos of your new cardamom cake." |
| 2 · One simple idea | a 10 second reel of the cake coming out of the oven |
| 3 · A short sample | the chai shop reel link |
| 4 · One easy question | "Can I make a free sample for you?" |

It is short, it is about them, and the question is easy to answer with yes.

### No reply? Follow up once

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | First message |
| Day 3 | One polite follow up |
| Then | Stop |

### Keep a simple sheet

Who you messaged, when, and what they said. The video's example:

| Who | When | What they said |
|---|---|---|
| Cafe | Day 1 | Yes, send a sample |
| Hair salon | Day 1 | No reply · follow up on Day 4 |
| Gym | Day 2 | Not right now |

Many people will not reply. That is normal. Keep going, and improve your message every week.

## Common mistakes

- **A long, copied message about you.** Make it short and about them.
- **Listing every service.** Offer one simple idea.
- **Following up again and again.** One polite follow up after three days, then stop.
- **Posting a free sample of a shop's product without asking.** Always get their permission first.
- **Setting a price without checking the site's fee page.**

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: write a first message for one real business near you, and send it.
2. Check your message against the four parts. Does it fit on one phone screen?
3. Start your sheet with three rows: who, when, and what they said.
