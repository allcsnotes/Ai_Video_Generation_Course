# M15-L04 · Pricing as a Beginner

> Video: Module 15, Lesson 4. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

You can make good videos now. So, what should you charge? Too low, and you work for nothing. Too high, and nobody says yes. You want a fair price that you can explain. This lesson shows what each video costs you, and simple ways to charge.

## What you'll learn

- The two costs of every video: credits and time
- Why you must count the clips that fail
- How to work out your **floor**: your lowest price
- Two ways to charge (per video, or a package) and extras
- How to write down what the price includes
- Beginner rules for getting paid

## Key ideas

### Know your cost: credits and time

Every video costs you two things: **credits** and **time**.

| Cost | From our builds |
|---|---|
| One Kling clip in our product ad | 30 credits |
| The talking shot in Flow | 18 credits |

- **Add the clips that fail.** For every clip you keep, plan for one or two that you throw away.
- **Time is the bigger cost.** Writing, making, editing, and changes can take **three or four hours**, even for one short video.

### Your floor price

Your **floor** is the lowest price you will take.

```text
Floor = hours the video takes × your rate for one hour + what the credits cost you
```

1. Decide what one hour of your work is worth.
2. Multiply it by the hours the video takes.
3. Add what the credits cost you.

These are example numbers only. Use your own. But never charge less than your floor.

### Two ways to charge, plus extras

| Way | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Per video | One price for one finished video | one 15 second reel |
| 2 · A package | A set of videos, a little less per video | four reels a month, a little less per reel |

With a package, the client saves money, and you get steady work.

**Extras cost extra:**

- a faster delivery
- more changes
- the same video in another size

### Write down what the price includes

Always write down exactly what the price includes. The video's example:

```text
One reel · what the price includes
- One video
- Up to 15 seconds
- 9:16, vertical
- Music and captions
- Two rounds of changes
A third round of changes: extra
```

A third round of changes costs extra. This stops a small job from growing forever.

### Getting paid

- As a beginner, **start a little low** to win your first clients.
- But after the first free sample, **do not work for free**.
- Ask for **half the payment before you start**, and the rest before you send the final file.
- After every few happy clients, **raise your price a little**.

## Worked example: the floor price

The video's example numbers:

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| Hours the video takes | 4 hours |
| Your rate for one hour | ₹300 |
| Time cost | 4 hours × ₹300 = ₹1,200 |
| Plus credits | + ₹200 for credits |
| **Your floor** | **₹1,400** |

So for this example, ₹1,400 is the lowest price. Anything below it means you are paying to work. Then the payment is split 50% before you start, 50% before the final file.

Use the [pricing worksheet](../../downloads/pricing-worksheet.md) to work out your own floor with your own numbers.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting only the clips you keep.** Plan for one or two failed clips for each kept clip.
- **Forgetting your time.** Time is the bigger cost: three or four hours even for one short video.
- **Charging below your floor** to win a job.
- **Not writing down what is included.** Then a small job grows forever.
- **Working for free after the first sample.**
- **Delivering before you are paid.** Take half first, and the rest before the final file.
