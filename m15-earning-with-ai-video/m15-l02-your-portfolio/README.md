# M15-L02 · Your Portfolio

> Video: Module 15, Lesson 2. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

A client will not read about your skills. They want to watch your work. A **portfolio** is a small set of videos that shows what you can do. This lesson covers which five videos to make, and how to show them.

## What you'll learn

- The five videos to put in your portfolio, one for each kind of client
- How to make them strong: length, first shot, glitches, and a one line note
- How to be honest about practice work
- Three ways to show your work: a showreel, a Google Drive folder, and a simple page
- How to share a Drive folder so anyone with the link can watch

## Key ideas

### Five videos, one for each kind of client

| # | Video | From |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A 15 second reel for Instagram | Build 1 |
| 2 | A product ad with a voice and music | Build 2 |
| 3 | A faceless vertical video for YouTube | Build 3 |
| 4 | A talking character, or a one minute short film | Builds 4 and 5 |
| 5 | An explainer with a voice and simple diagrams | Build 7 |

You already made all five in Modules 12 and 13. Pick your best version of each.

### Make them strong

- **Keep each one short**, between 15 and 60 seconds.
- **Put your best shot in the first two seconds.** The client decides fast.
- **Fix every glitch you can see.** One bad hand, or a broken label, makes a client doubt the whole set.
- **Add a one line note under each**: what it is, which tools you used, and how long it took.

The video's example note:

```text
Sample ad · Kling, Shotcut · 2 hours
```

### Be honest about your work

- If you made it for practice, for a brand you invented like Chai Corner, **call it a sample**. Never pretend it was a paid job.
- Never use a real brand's logo without permission, and never show someone else's work as yours.
- Keep the AI label wherever the platform asks for it.

### Three ways to show your work

1. **A showreel.** One video, 30 to 60 seconds long, with the best moments from all five. It is perfect for a quick first message.
2. **A Google Drive folder** with the five full videos.
3. **A simple page** with your name, your five videos, what you offer, and how to contact you. A free page builder is enough. Keep it clean, and put the showreel at the top.

## Worked example: share the Drive folder

The video shows Google Drive's help page, "Share with a link".

### 1. Make the folder

Create one folder in Google Drive and put the five full videos in it.

### 2. Open sharing

Select the folder, and click **Share**.

### 3. Change who can open it

Under **General access**, choose **Anyone with the link**.

### 4. Copy the link

Copy the link. Now anyone with the link can watch, without signing in.

```text
Google Drive > select the folder > Share > General access > Anyone with the link > Copy link
```

Test it: open the link in a private browser window. If the videos play without signing in, it works.

## Common mistakes

- **Long videos.** Keep each between 15 and 60 seconds, best shot first.
- **One visible glitch.** A bad hand or a broken label makes the client doubt the whole set. Fix it or leave that video out.
- **Calling practice work a paid job.** Call it a sample.
- **A real brand's logo in a sample.** Use an invented brand, like Chai Corner.
- **A Drive link that asks people to sign in.** Set General access to Anyone with the link.
