### Why start from a picture?

| Words only | Start from a picture |
|---|---|
| Kling invents a new person every time you generate (same words, two different women) | You decide how the person and the place look. Kling only adds the movement |

And pictures are cheap to fix. Get the picture right in ChatGPT first, then spend video credits once.

## Follow along

### Step 1 · Open Generate and add your picture

1. Open **Generate**, and click the **+** button in the prompt box.
2. Pick **Upload**, and choose your picture from Module 4.

What you should see: your picture shows up as a small picture inside the box. Keep **Omni reference**. Kling now uses your picture as the guide.

Click path: `Generate > + > Upload > your picture`

### Step 2 · Write only the movement

Here is the big change. Do not describe the picture again. Kling already sees it.

| Do not repeat the picture | Write what changes |
|---|---|
| "A young woman with wavy black hair, a white shirt and a yellow apron, standing at a chai stall in warm light..." | Movement: keeps pouring, steam rises, looks up and smiles, hair moves a little |
| Kling already sees all of this | Camera: slow push in |
| | Protect her: keep her face and clothes the same, no text |

The full prompt is in "Prompts used" below.

### Step 3 · Check the settings

Open the settings.

- The ratio is already **9:16**, the same shape as the picture.
- Keep **5 seconds** and **one output**.

The button says **30** credits, the same as text to video.

### Step 4 · Press Generate, once

Press **Generate** once. **This spends 30 credits.** In the video, 582 became 552, and the clip waits in the queue.

Click path: `Generate > + > Upload > Video Generation > Kling 4.0 Flash > Omni reference > 720p · 5 s · 9:16 > Generate`

### Step 5 · Check the result

| Check | The video's clip |
|---|---|
| Movement? | She pours, the steam rises, and she looks up and smiles. Yes |
| Same face? | The same woman from start to end. Same hair, same earrings, same apron. Yes |
| First frame? | Very close to your picture, but not exact. Her eyes look down, and the camera is a little closer |

That is how Omni reference works: it follows your picture as a guide. To lock the exact first frame, use **Keyframes**. That is the next lesson.

## Prompts used

```text
The woman in the yellow apron keeps pouring a thin stream of hot chai from the kettle into the glass. Steam rises from the glass. She looks up at the camera and smiles warmly. Her hair moves a little in the breeze. Slow push in. Keep her face and clothes the same. No text.
```

The pattern:

```text
[Who, in a few words] [what moves]. [Small detail that moves]. [Camera move]. Keep [her / his] face and clothes the same. No text.
```

## Common mistakes

1. **Describing the picture again.** Kling already sees it. Write only the movement.
2. **The wrong shape.** A wide picture makes a wide clip. For Reels, make a 9:16 picture first.
3. **A weak picture.** Strange hands or a blurry face only get worse when they move. Fix the picture first.

The fix: a strong 9:16 picture, and words only about movement.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: bring your best picture from Module 4 to life, and check that the face stays the same from start to end.
2. Before you generate, cross out every word in your prompt that only describes what is already in the picture.
3. Compare the first frame of your clip with your picture. What is different?

## Recap

- Start from a strong picture. You decide the look, and Kling adds the movement.
- Upload it with the plus button, and keep Omni reference.
- In the prompt, write only the movement and the camera, and keep the face the same.
