# M03-L01 · Setting Up Claude as Your Director

> Video: Module 3, Lesson 1. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Every AI video starts with words. Before ChatGPT makes an image or Kling makes a clip, someone has to plan the idea, the shots and the prompts. In this course, Claude does that planning.

But a normal chat starts empty. So every time you would type the same rules again: your tools, your format, how long each shot is. Again, and again. Today you fix that once, by setting up Claude as your own video director.

## What you'll learn

- What a Claude **Project** is: instructions, context and chats
- Why a project saves you from typing the same brief every time
- How to create the AI Video Director project, step by step
- The three-part director instruction: role, steps and rules
- How to test it with one line, and what a good answer looks like
- What to do if your plan has no Projects

## Before you start

- You need a Claude account at `claude.ai`. Sign in with your own account; never share your password with anyone.
- Nothing in this lesson spends credits. Claude is used for words only.
- If your Claude plan does not show **Projects**, you can still follow along: keep the instruction in a notes file and paste it at the top of each new chat (see the end of this page).

### What is a Project?

In Claude, a **Project** is like a folder for one kind of work. It holds three things:

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Instructions | The rules Claude reads before every answer |
| Context | Files you want Claude to know, like notes about your brand |
| Chats | Every conversation you start inside the project |

The key idea: every new chat inside the project reads the same instructions first. So you write your rules once, and Claude follows them every time. Think of a film set: the director gives the crew one brief, and everyone follows it. Your instructions are that brief.

### Without vs with a Project

In a normal chat, every message needs the full brief:

```text
I use ChatGPT for images and Kling for video. Make it 9:16. Split it into 6-second shots. Write an image prompt and a video prompt for each shot. Idea: a chai stall that opens at 7 AM.
```

Forget one rule, and the answer changes. Inside the project you type only the idea, in one line. The rules are already there.

## Follow along

### Step 1 · Open Claude

In your browser, go to `claude.ai` and sign in.

### Step 2 · Projects

Click path: `left menu > Projects`

What you should see: a page that lists your projects.

### Step 3 · New project

Click **New project**, at the top right.

### Step 4 · Name it

Name: `AI Video Director`

Below the name, write what it is for. On screen:

```text
Plan, script and write prompts for my short AI videos (Reels and YouTube).
```

### Step 5 · Create

Click **Create project**.

What you should see: your project is ready. On the right you see **Instructions**, **Context** and **Scheduled**. Instructions is the one that matters today.

### Step 6 · Add instructions

Next to **Instructions**, click **Add**. A box called "Set project instructions" opens.

### Step 7 · Paste the director instruction and save

The instruction has three parts:

| Part | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 · Role | "You are my AI video director", plus the tools: ChatGPT, Kling, Veo, CapCut |
| 2 · Steps | Ask platform and length · script with a 2-second hook · split into shots of 5 to 8 s · image + video prompt per shot |
| 3 · Rules | Simple English · 9:16 vertical · no text in the video · same character every shot |

Why shots of 5 to 8 seconds? Because Kling and Veo make short clips. So one shot is one clip.

Why no text inside the video? AI is still weak at text, so we add it in CapCut.

Why describe the main character the same way every time? So the person looks the same in every clip.

Pause the video and copy the instruction (it is below, in Prompts used) into your own project. Then click **Save instructions**.

### Step 8 · Test it

What you should see first: next to Instructions it now says **Edit** instead of Add. The rules are saved.

In the project chat, type one line:

```text
Idea: a 30-second Instagram Reel for a small chai stall that opens at 7 AM.
```

What came back in the video, from that one line:

1. **Format and mood:** 9:16 vertical, 5 shots of 6 seconds each, warm and calm.
2. **The same character:** a character description to reuse in every shot. That is the rule, working.
3. **A script** with a hook in the first seconds, and the text to add later in CapCut.
4. **Every shot:** how close the camera is, how it moves and the light, with an image prompt for ChatGPT and a video prompt for Kling and Veo.
5. **Tips** at the end.

The character Claude wrote, which the next lessons reuse:

```text
a friendly Indian man in his mid-50s, short salt-and-pepper beard, thin black-rimmed glasses, cream cotton half-sleeve kurta, red-and-white checked cotton towel on his left shoulder
```

The course builds this exact chai video in the coming lessons.

## Prompts used

The project instruction (Step 7), exactly as typed in the video:

```text
You are my AI video director. I make short videos with AI: ChatGPT for images, Kling and Veo for video clips, CapCut for editing.

When I give you an idea:
1. If I did not say, ask the platform (Reels, Shorts or YouTube) and the length.
2. Write a short script. The hook must grab attention in the first 2 seconds.
3. Split it into numbered shots of 5 to 8 seconds. For each shot give the shot size, camera move, light and mood.
4. For every shot, write one image prompt and one video prompt.

Rules:
- Simple English, short sentences.
- 9:16 vertical unless I say otherwise.
- No text or logos inside the video. We add text in CapCut.
- Describe the main character the same way in every shot.
```

The test (Step 8):

```text
Idea: a 30-second Instagram Reel for a small chai stall that opens at 7 AM.
```

## Common mistakes

- **Vague rules, like "make it good".** Claude cannot measure good. Use numbers: 9:16, five shots of 6 seconds.
- **A huge instruction.** Three pages of rules is too much. Keep it short enough to fit on one screen, or the important rules get lost.
- **Never updating it.** When Claude keeps making the same mistake, add one new rule.

Clear, short, and updated when needed.

## Your task

Create your own AI Video Director project, paste the instruction, and test it with your own idea. If the answer follows your rules (the format, one character, a hook, shots with two prompts each), it works.

No Projects on your plan? Keep the instruction in a notes file, and paste it at the top of each new chat.
