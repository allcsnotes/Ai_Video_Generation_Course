# M09-L01 · Why Faces Change

> Video: Module 9, Lesson 1. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

One prompt, run twice in Flow: two clips, and two different women. Same words, same settings, but a new face, a new stall, a new everything. This lesson explains why, and shows the fix.

## What you'll learn

- Why the same words give a new face on every run
- Why clothes and places drift too when a detail is missing
- The fix: a start picture of your character
- Three rules for keeping a character the same
- Why you should always check the last second of a clip

## Key ideas

### Words fit millions of faces

Read the prompt again: a young Indian woman, wavy hair, a white shirt, a yellow apron. Millions of real women fit those words. The words do not point to one face.

Every time you press generate, the model starts from new **random noise** (the random static a video model starts from, as in Module 1) and finds a new face that fits. You saw this with pictures in Module 4: the same prompt, twice, gave two different women.

### The real test

The prompt in Flow: 9:16, 360p, 5 seconds, outputs set to **x2**. x2 makes two clips from one prompt, so this cost **10 credits**.

| Check | Run 1 vs run 2 |
|---|---|
| wavy hair | both |
| white shirt | both |
| yellow apron | both |
| a kettle | both |
| the face | different |

Both match the words. But the faces belong to two different people. Put these two clips in one ad, and your viewer sees two different women.

### Clothes and places drift too

A clip from Module 7 came from a short prompt that only said: "A woman at her chai stall". So the model guessed the rest: a yellow kurta, a black apron and a busy market. Nobody asked for any of these.

When a detail is missing, the model fills the gap with a guess. And it guesses again every time.

### The fix: a picture

Give the model a **start frame** of your character. The same prompt, word for word; the only change is Meera's picture in the **Start** box. Same settings, x2 again, 10 credits.

Now both clips show the same woman: same face, same hair, same earrings, same apron.

| The picture tells the model | The words tell the model |
|---|---|
| **who she is** | only **what she does** |

### Three rules

1. **One master picture.** Make one master picture of your character, like Meera's sheet from Module 4, and start every shot from it.
2. **The same description.** Copy the same description, word for word, into every prompt. Never write it fresh. For example: `A young Indian woman, shoulder-length wavy dark hair, white shirt, mustard yellow apron`
3. **Never fight the picture.** In Module 7, a prompt asked for a green saree at a night market, and her clothes jumped.

### Check the last second

Even with a picture, the face can drift a little near the end.

In the video's clip, at 0.2 seconds the face matches; at 4.9 seconds it has shifted a little and steam covers it.

If it drifts, cut that part, or make it again.

## Worked example

Both tests use the same prompt, at 9:16, 360p, 5 s, x2 (10 credits each test).

```text
A young Indian woman with shoulder-length wavy dark hair, a white shirt and a mustard yellow apron, smiles and pours chai from a steel kettle into a glass at her street chai stall. Warm morning light.
```

| Test | Start box | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Words only | empty | two different women |
| With a picture | Meera's picture | the same woman twice: same face, hair, earrings, apron (a little drift near the end) |

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting the words to fix a face.** Words fit millions of faces; only a picture points to one.
- **Leaving out details.** A missing detail gets a new guess every time.
- **Rewriting the description for each shot.** Copy and paste it word for word.
- **Asking for a new look with a start picture.** The text fights the picture and the clip jumps.
- **Not checking the end of the clip.** The face can drift in the last second.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: make two clips of your character, one from words only and one from a picture. Put them side by side and compare the faces.
2. Write a fixed description for your own character in one line (age, hair, clothes), ready to paste into every prompt.
3. Look at the last second of one of your clips. Did the face drift? What would you do?

## Answers

1. Good looks like: the words-only clip shows a different face from your character; the picture clip shows the same face as your picture.
2. Follow the video's pattern: `A young Indian woman, shoulder-length wavy dark hair, white shirt, mustard yellow apron`.
3. If it drifted, cut that part in the edit, or make the clip again.
