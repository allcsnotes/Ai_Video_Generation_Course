# M09-L04 · Continuity Between Cuts

> Video: Module 9, Lesson 4. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Meera walks to her stall, moving to the right. Then the video cuts. Now she comes in from the right, walking left. Did she turn around? It feels like a jump, even though every frame looks fine. **Continuity** means the things that carry across a cut stay the same, so two shots feel like one moment.

## What you'll learn

- Why the viewer joins two shots into one moment, and notices any jump
- The three things that must carry across a cut: direction, light and time of day
- How to keep the direction of movement the same (out to the right, in from the left)
- How to keep the light on the same side, and the time of day the same
- How to check every cut before you keep a shot

## Key ideas

### The viewer joins the cut

When two shots are cut together, the viewer joins them into one moment. So anything that jumps between them gets noticed, even when each shot is good on its own.

| # | What carries across | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Direction | she keeps moving the same way |
| 2 | Light | the sun stays on the same side |
| 3 | Time of day | morning stays morning |

### Direction

Shot one is a wide shot: Meera walks from the left of the frame to the right, carrying a tray to her stall. It uses the same ingredients as the last lesson (Meera and the stall picture) and the same fixed blocks for her, the place and the look.

She leaves this shot moving right. So in the next shot she should still move right, **coming in from the left**. Same way across the cut = one walk.

### The test: the next shot, made twice

The next shot is a medium shot at her counter, made twice. Only a few words changed:

| Version | The words | Result after shot one |
|---|---|---|
| A | from the left side, moving to the right | the walk flows on |
| B | from the right side, moving to the left | she seems to turn around |

Each one cost **5 credits**, and Flow followed both directions. Watch each one right after shot one: from the left, the walk flows on; from the right, she seems to turn around.

### Light

The look line says: **warm golden sunlight coming from the left**. In the walk and in the match, the bright haze sits on the left, just as written.

If the light jumps sides between cuts, the scene looks pasted together.

### Time of day

Every shot here is early morning. A blue evening shot in the middle would break it. Change the time only on purpose, at the start of a new part, and then keep it.

### Check the cut

Before you keep a shot, play the end of one shot, then the start of the next. Ask three questions:

1. Is she moving the same way?
2. Is the light on the same side?
3. Is it the same time of day?

If any answer is no, fix those words in the prompt and make it again. Five credits is cheaper than a broken scene.

And if she really must change direction, show her turning **inside one shot**, so the viewer sees why.

## Common mistakes

- **Judging each shot alone.** A shot can look fine and still break the cut. Play the end of one and the start of the next.
- **Flipping the direction.** Out to the right means in from the left in the next shot.
- **Light from a new side.** Keep the light words ("coming from the left") in the fixed look block.
- **A different time of day in the middle.** Change time only on purpose, at the start of a new part.
- **An unexplained turn.** If she must change direction, show the turn inside one shot.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: make two shots of someone walking, cut them together, and check all three things: direction, light and time of day.
2. Shot one ends with your character walking out to the left. Which side should they come in from in the next shot?
3. Your look line says the sun comes from the right. Write the light words for the next shot.

## Answers

1. Good looks like: three "yes" answers when you play the end of shot one and the start of shot two.
2. From the right, still moving left.
3. Keep the same words in the look block, for example: `warm golden sunlight coming from the right`.
