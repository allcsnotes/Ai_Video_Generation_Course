# M06-L03 · Character and Product

> Video: Module 6, Lesson 3. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

So far, Meera and the Chai Corner tin lived in separate clips. A real ad needs both: your character, holding your product. Today Kling gets two pictures at once, one of Meera and one of the tin, and puts them together in one scene.

## What you'll learn

- The two jobs a picture can do in Kling: first frame or reference
- How to add two reference pictures with Omni reference
- How to name each picture in the prompt (Image1, Image2) and give each a job
- Why the first try cut away to a product-only shot, and the one-line fix
- Three mistakes with two pictures

## Before you start

- **Account:** your free Kling account.
- **Two pictures:** your character (the video uses Meera, the steam-free picture from M05-L06) and your product (the Chai Corner tin from M04-L05, on a plain background).
- **Cost:** each try is Kling 4.0 Flash, 720p, 5 seconds, 9:16: **30 credits**. The video needed two tries: **60 credits** in total. Prices change: read the number on the Generate button before you press it.

### A picture has two jobs

| Job | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1 · First frame | The exact start of the clip. The clip's frame 1 is the very same picture |
| 2 · Reference | It only tells Kling who or what to show. Kling builds a brand new scene around it: same person, new pose and shot |

**Omni reference** takes several reference pictures:

- Picture 1 is the **who**: Meera.
- Picture 2 is the **what**: the tin.
- Your prompt is the **scene**. Kling puts them together in the scene your prompt describes.

## Follow along

### Step 1 · Set up the menus

Open **Generate**, with **Video Generation** and **Kling 4.0 Flash**. Set the mode to **Omni reference**.

### Step 2 · Add the character

Click the **+** button, then **Upload**, and pick the picture of Meera.

### Step 3 · Add the product

Click **+** again, and upload the tin. Both pictures now sit in a small stack.

### Step 4 · Name each picture

In the prompt box, type the email symbol, the little "a" in a circle: **@**. A list of your pictures opens: **Image1** and **Image2**.

Pick one, and it becomes a small tag inside your words. Now each picture has a name.

### Step 5 · Give each name a job

| Name | Its job |
|---|---|
| Image1 | the who: she does the action. Stands at her chai stall, holds up the tin from Image2 next to her face, then taps the lid once |
| Image2 | the what: the product she holds |

Then two lines that keep things the same:

- Keep her face and clothes the same as Image1.
- Keep the tin and its label exactly like Image2.

The full prompt is in "Prompts used" below. The button says **30**. Press **Generate**. **This spends 30 credits.**

Click path: `Generate > Video Generation > Kling 4.0 Flash > Omni reference > + > Upload (Meera) > + > Upload (tin) > type @ to tag Image1 and Image2 > Generate`

### Step 6 · Check try 1

Watch all five seconds.

- **The start is great.** Her face matches, and the tin and its label match too.
- **Then it cuts away.** Halfway through (at about 2.5 seconds), the clip cuts to a shot of only the tin, on a table. Kling copied picture two as a scene of its own.

It saw a clean product photo and made a product shot. Nothing in the prompt said to stay with Meera.

### Step 7 · The fix: one more line

Click **Edit**, and the pictures and the prompt come back. At the end, add:

```text
One continuous shot from start to end. She holds the tin the whole time.
```

We do not write "do not cut". Just like the clear air in Module 5, we say what we want instead.

Press **Generate**. **This spends another 30 credits.**

### Step 8 · Check try 2

One shot, from start to end, with no cut. She lifts the tin next to her face, touches the lid, and the camera slowly pushes in.

Two tries cost 60 credits. So read your prompt once more before you press Generate.

## Prompts used

Try 1 (each `@Image` is a tag you pick from the @ list):

```text
@Image1 stands at her chai stall and holds up the @Image2 tin next to her face and smiles warmly. Then she taps the lid once. The camera slowly pushes in. Keep her face and clothes the same as @Image1. Keep the tin and its label exactly like @Image2. No extra text.
```

Try 2 (the same prompt plus one line):

```text
@Image1 stands at her chai stall and holds up the @Image2 tin next to her face and smiles warmly. Then she taps the lid once. The camera slowly pushes in. Keep her face and clothes the same as @Image1. Keep the tin and its label exactly like @Image2. No extra text. One continuous shot from start to end. She holds the tin the whole time.
```

## Common mistakes

1. **A busy product photo.** A table and props in the picture can end up in the clip. A plain background is safer.
2. **No names.** Write "the woman" and "the tin", and Kling may mix them up. Use Image1 and Image2.
3. **Too many pictures.** Each extra picture is one more thing to match. Start with two.

The fix: a plain background, names for each picture, two pictures to start.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: put your own character and your own product together in one five second clip, one continuous shot. Check the face and the label.
2. Before you generate, read your prompt. Does it name each picture, give each a job, and say "one continuous shot"?
3. Your product photo shows the bottle on a table with flowers. What could go wrong, and how do you fix it before spending credits?

## Recap

- Omni reference uses pictures as the who and the what, not as the first frame.
- Upload your character and your product, then name them in the prompt with the email symbol.
- Give each picture a job, and say what you want: one continuous shot.
