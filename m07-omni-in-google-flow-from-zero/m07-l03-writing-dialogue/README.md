# M07-L03 · Writing Dialogue

> Video: Module 7, Lesson 3. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

In the last lesson a voice spoke off screen. Now you put a person on screen and make her say your line. Omni can make a face speak, with the lips moving to the words, all from one prompt.

## What you'll learn

- What a lazy prompt gives you: a script you never wrote
- The four things every dialogue prompt needs: who speaks, the exact line, how it sounds, no subtitles
- How to get your line word for word, with lips that match
- How to make two people talk in the right order with "first" and "then"
- Four rules for dialogue

## Before you start

- Flow with Omni 1.2 Flash, Agent off, as in lesson 1.
- Every test uses **9:16 · 360p · 8 s · x1 = 8 credits**. The video makes three tests, so **24 credits** in all.
- **Dialogue** means the words a person on screen says.

## Key ideas

### A lazy prompt

The first test is one short line with no exact words, no look and no place:

```text
A woman at her chai stall talks to the camera about her chai.
```

She talks, and her lips move. But listen to the words:

> "There's nothing like a fresh cup of chai made with real ginger and fresh cardamom, take a sip and feel the warmth"

Ginger, cardamom, feel the warmth: nobody wrote that. The model wrote its own script. It also chose her clothes (a yellow kurta with a black apron) and the place (a busy market).

### The dialogue formula: four things

| # | Part | Example from the video |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who speaks: age, clothes, where she stands | a young Indian woman, about 25, in a green cotton saree, behind her chai stall |
| 2 | The exact line, inside quotes | "Welcome to Chai Corner. The best masala chai in town!" |
| 3 | How it sounds: tone and accent | in a warm, friendly voice with a light Indian accent |
| 4 | No subtitles, so the words are not printed on screen | No subtitles. No text. |

### The full prompt

A young woman in a green saree, behind her stall at sunrise. She smiles and says, in a warm, friendly voice with a light Indian accent: "Welcome to Chai Corner. The best masala chai in town!" (The full prompt is in the Worked example below.)

Result, same settings: word for word, the line you wrote, and her lips move with the words. The prompt also said green saree and sunrise, and that is what you get.

### Two people

A customer asks, and she answers.

1. Give each person **a place and a look**: on the left, the woman in green; on the right, an older man in a grey shirt.
2. Set the order with **first** and **then**: first the man says "One chai, please." Then the woman says "Coming right up!"

Result: the deep voice comes first, then hers. Each line comes from the right person, in the right order.

| Order | Who | Voice | Line |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | The man | deep, tired voice | "One chai, please." |
| Then | The woman | warm, cheerful voice | "Coming right up!" |

### Four rules for dialogue

1. **Keep it short.** About ten words fit in 8 seconds, for everyone together. Count the words before you click.
2. **A different look and a different voice for each speaker**, so the model can tell them apart (for example: an older man, grey shirt, deep voice).
3. **First, then.** The order you write is the order they speak.
4. **No subtitles and no music**, unless you want them.

## Worked example

All three prompts at 9:16, 360p, 8 s, x1 (8 credits each).

One person, the full prompt:

```text
Medium close-up of a young Indian woman, about 25, in a green cotton saree, standing behind her small roadside chai stall at sunrise. She looks at the camera, smiles and says in a warm, friendly voice with a light Indian accent: "Welcome to Chai Corner. The best masala chai in town!" Her lips move clearly with the words. Background: a quiet street, birds far away. No music. No subtitles. No text.
```

Two people:

```text
Medium shot at a small roadside chai stall at sunrise. On the left, a young Indian woman, about 25, in a green cotton saree, stands behind the counter. On the right, an older man in a grey shirt walks up to the counter. First the man says in a deep, tired voice: "One chai, please." Then the woman smiles and says in a warm, cheerful voice: "Coming right up!" She starts pouring the chai. Background: a quiet street, birds far away. No music. No subtitles. No text.
```

What came out:

- Lazy prompt: the model's own line, its own clothes and place.
- Full prompt: the exact line, lips that match, green saree, sunrise.
- Two people: "One chai, please." in the deep voice first, then "Coming right up!" in hers, and she pours.

## Common mistakes

- **A lazy prompt.** No exact words means the model writes its own script.
- **A line that is too long.** About ten words, for everyone together, in 8 seconds.
- **Two speakers who look alike.** Give each a different look and a different voice.
- **No order.** Without "first" and "then", the lines can come in the wrong order.
- **Forgetting "No subtitles".** The model may print the words on the screen.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: make one person say one short line of your own, in 8 seconds (360p, about 10 words).
2. Run the lazy prompt above once and write down what the model chose for you: the words, the clothes, the place.
3. Write a two-person prompt for your own idea with a place, a look and a voice for each, and "first" and "then".

## Answers

1. Good looks like: the line is in quotes, about 10 words or fewer; you hear it word for word and the lips match.
2. Expect a line, clothes and a place you did not write. That is why each one goes in the prompt.
3. Check: each person has a side (left, right), a look and a voice; the lines together are about 10 words; "first" and "then" set the order.
