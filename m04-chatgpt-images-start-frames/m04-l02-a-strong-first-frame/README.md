# M04-L02 · A Strong First Frame

> Video: Module 4, Lesson 2. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Two start frames for our chai video. One is busy and full of life. The other is plain and simple. Which one makes the better video? The plain one. In a video, everything in the frame has to move. More things means more mistakes.

## What you'll learn

- Why a busy first frame breaks when it starts to move
- The three rules of a strong first frame: one clear subject, room for the motion, a plain background
- A reusable prompt formula for a strong first frame
- Why the AI places things close to where you ask, not exactly
- Three first-frame mistakes and how to avoid them

## Key ideas

### Why the busy frame breaks

In a video, every thing in the frame has to move. Each one is a chance for a mistake:

| In the busy frame | What goes wrong when it moves |
|---|---|
| Signs full of letters | Letters wobble and turn into nonsense |
| A crowd of faces | Small faces in the back often melt or change |
| Cups, pots and scooters | Each one can flicker, bend or vanish |

The plain frame has only three things: one man, one pot and one glass. Fewer things, fewer mistakes.

### Rule 1 · One clear subject

Put him a little to one side, not dead in the centre. (Think of the frame split into three columns, the "thirds": the subject stands on the left or the right third.)

### Rule 2 · Leave room for the motion

The chai will pour from the pot into the glass. So the glass waits on the right, with empty space above it. Leave empty space where the action goes.

### Rule 3 · A plain background

Soft blur, no signs, no people and no text.

One subject, room to move, and a plain background. That is a strong first frame.

### The strong first frame formula

You can reuse the strong prompt as a formula. Fill in the blanks:

| Part | What you write |
|---|---|
| Subject | [who], [what they look like] |
| Place | stands on the [left / right] third of the frame |
| Motion room | On the other side, [target], with empty space for [the action] |
| Background | Plain, softly blurred background, no signs, no people. |
| Shot · light · shape | [shot], [light]. Vertical 9:16, no text. |

Always end with: no text.

### An honest note: close, not exact

The AI places things close, not exactly. In the video's result, the man is nearer the centre than we asked. Close is fine, as long as the motion has room.

## Worked example

Both prompts were typed into ChatGPT in the video.

**The busy prompt.** We asked for clutter on purpose:

```text
Create an image of a chai seller at his very busy roadside stall, lots of customers, colourful signs and posters everywhere, many pots, cups and snacks, scooters passing, vertical 9:16.
```

What came out: a busy stall, signs full of letters, and our man lost in the middle.

**The strong prompt.** It starts with the same description of our chai man, then follows the three rules:

```text
Create an image. The chai man: a friendly Indian man in his mid-50s, short salt-and-pepper beard, thin black-rimmed glasses, cream cotton kurta, red-and-white checked towel on his left shoulder. He stands on the left third of the frame and holds a steel pot high. On the right, a small glass waits on the counter with empty space above it for the chai to pour. Plain, softly blurred background, no signs, no people. Medium shot, eye level. Warm morning light from the right. Photorealistic, vertical 9:16, no text.
```

How it maps to the rules:

| Rule | The words in the prompt |
|---|---|
| 1 · Place him | on the left third of the frame, holds a steel pot high |
| 2 · Room for motion | on the right, a small glass ... with empty space above it for the chai to pour |
| 3 · Plain background | plain, softly blurred background, no signs, no people |
| Shot, light, shape | medium shot, eye level, warm morning light from the right, vertical 9:16, no text |

What came out: one man, one pot, one glass, and a clear path for the pour. He is a little nearer the centre than asked, but the pour still has room.

## Common mistakes

1. **The action goes out of the frame.** If the glass is cut off, the pour has nowhere to land.
2. **Framing too tight.** If the steel pot touches the top edge, there is no room for it to move.
3. **A busy background with signs.** Every extra thing is one more thing to go wrong.

The fix: keep the action inside the frame, leave space, keep it plain.

## Recap

- In a video, everything in the frame moves. So fewer things means fewer mistakes.
- Use one clear subject to one side, room for the motion, and a plain background with no text.
- Write all three into your image prompt, with the formula.
