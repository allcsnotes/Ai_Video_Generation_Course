# M10-L03 · Music

> Video: Module 10, Lesson 3. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

Same shots, same voice, now with music under it. That music was free, and safe to use in an ad, with no credit needed. This lesson shows where to find tracks like that, and how to avoid copyright claims and strikes.

## What you'll learn

- Why a famous song under your ad is a problem: Content ID, claims and strikes
- How to find free music on Pixabay, with the right filters
- What the Pixabay Content License lets you do, and what it does not
- The shield icon: the one check that saves you from YouTube claims
- How to cut a long track to fit a short ad, with soft fades

## Before you start

- A browser. Pixabay music is free, and you do not even need an account to browse and download.
- Nothing in this lesson spends credits.
- Your ad from the last lesson: three shots, one background and the voiceover (about 12 seconds).

### Why it matters

Say you put a famous film song under your chai ad.

| What happens | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Content ID** | YouTube checks every upload with this system. It finds the song in seconds |
| **A claim** | The owner picks one: take the money your video makes, mute it, or block it |
| **A strike** | Worse: the owner asks YouTube to take your video down. 3 strikes in 90 days, and your channel is removed |

Instagram and Facebook check music too. A song you love is still owned by someone.

## Follow along

### Step 1 · Open Pixabay music

Go to `pixabay.com/music`. No account needed.

### Step 2 · Search a mood or an instrument

The video typed `santoor`, for a calm Indian morning.

### Step 3 · Use the filters

- **Mood:** like relaxing or bright.
- **Duration:** so the track is long enough for your ad.

### Step 4 · Content type

Choose only music made by people, or only music made with AI.

### Step 5 · Open a track, play, download

Open a track and press play. Like it? Click **Free download**, and you get an MP3 file. The video picked **Santoor Sunrise** (3:00 long).

### Step 6 · Read the license

Every track says: free for use under the **Pixabay Content License**.

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Use the music for free | Sell the track on its own |
| No credit needed | Claim it as your own song |
| Cut it and change it | |

### Step 7 · Keep proof

Save three things in your project folder (the video uses a folder called `Chai Corner ad / music proof`):

| Proof | Example from the video |
|---|---|
| The track link | `pixabay.com/music/india-santoor-sunrise-627837` |
| The file name | The MP3 you downloaded, not renamed |
| The license page | `pixabay.com/service/license-summary` |

If anyone ever asks, you can show where the music came from.

### Step 8 · Check for the shield

Look at the small icons next to each track's length.

| Icon | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Shield** | The track is registered with Content ID. YouTube may flag your video, even when you used the track legally |
| **Robot** | The music was made with AI |

A claim is not a strike, and you can dispute it with your proof. But it takes time, and your earnings can be held while you wait. The easy way: **pick a track with no shield**. Santoor Sunrise has no shield, only the little robot.

### Step 9 · Fit the music to the ad

The track is 3 minutes long. The ad is about 12 seconds.

1. Pick a calm part, with no big changes. The video took a part from **0:10**, near the start: **12.6 seconds**.
2. **Fade in over 0.5 s**, and **fade out over 1.5 s**, so it never stops suddenly.
3. Keep the music well below the voice. The next lesson sets those levels properly.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: find a free track for your ad with no shield. Save the link and the license page, and add the two fades.
2. Search two different moods for the same ad, and note which tracks have a shield.
3. Pick a calm part of your track that is just as long as your ad.

What good looks like: a track with no shield, a folder with the link, the unrenamed MP3 and the license page, and music that fades in over half a second and out over one and a half.

## Common mistakes

- **Using a famous song.** It can get your video claimed, muted, or struck.
- **Not reading the license.** Free to use does not mean you can resell the track on its own.
- **Ignoring the shield.** A Content ID track can bring a claim even when you used it legally.
- **Throwing away your proof.** Keep the link, the file name and the license page.
- **Starting the music on a big change, or stopping it dead.** Use a calm part and soft fades.
