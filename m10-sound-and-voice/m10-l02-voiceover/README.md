# M10-L02 · Voiceover

> Video: Module 10, Lesson 2. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

The video opens with three shots, one background, and a voice on top. That voice was free: it came from a free **text to speech** tool (a tool that turns typed words into a spoken voice), in about 10 seconds.

## What you'll learn

- How to record your own voice with just a phone
- How to make a free voiceover in Google AI Studio's Generate speech
- The trick that changes everything: the Style box
- When you need lip sync, and the two ways to get it
- How to fit the voice to the picture by counting words

## Before you start

- **Your own voice:** a phone with its voice recorder app. Nothing to pay.
- **Text to speech:** Google AI Studio, in the browser, with your Google account. The video says it is free to use; no credits are spent in this lesson. Free tools can change, so check what is free today.
- Your three muted shots and one background from the last lesson.

## Follow along

### Way 1 · Record your own voice

| Tip | Why |
|---|---|
| Your phone's voice recorder app | It is enough |
| A small, quiet room with soft things: curtains, a bed, clothes | Soft things stop the echo |
| Phone about a hand's width (about 15 cm) from your mouth, a little to the side | Your breath goes past it, not into it |
| Record 5 seconds of silence first | Then start your lines |
| Say each line twice | Keep the best take |

### Way 2 · Free text to speech

No quiet room, or not happy with your voice? Use free text to speech.

### Step 1 · Open Generate speech

Click path: `Google AI Studio > Generate speech`

What you should see: the Generate speech page, with a box for your words.

### Step 2 · Pick a voice

There are more than 2,000 voices. Filter them by **language, gender, pitch and style**, and pick one.

### Step 3 · Type your line and click Run

Type your line, and click **Run**. In about 10 seconds you can play it and download it.

The line used in the video:

```text
Every morning at Chai Corner, the kettle starts before the sun. Fresh ginger, real cardamom, and a smile in every glass.
```

### Step 4 · The trick: the Style box

Click **Style**, and describe how the voice should sound. The video wrote:

```text
Warm and calm, like a friendly chai seller at sunrise. Slow pace, a small smile in the voice.
```

Then Run again and compare:

| Take | Length |
|---|---|
| No style | 8.6 seconds |
| Warm and calm, slow pace | 12.1 seconds |

Same words, a warmer voice, and much slower: about eight and a half seconds became twelve.

### Step 5 · Decide on lip sync

| Where the voice is | What you need |
|---|---|
| Off screen, over shots of the chai and the stall | No lip sync. Nothing to match. The easy path |
| A person speaks on camera | The lips must match |

Two ways for on camera:

1. Write the line in an **Omni** prompt, so she says it herself (Module 7).
2. Take your audio into **Kling Lip Sync** (Module 6).

Tip: keep talking on camera short. One or two lines, then cut to the product.

### Step 6 · Fit the voice to the picture: count your words

| Pace | Words a second |
|---|---|
| Normal voice | about 2.5 |
| Our slow, warm voice | 1.7 (fewer than two) |

- A **15 second** ad holds about **35 words** at a normal pace.
- Use fewer words for a slow, gentle voice.
- Leave **half a second (0.5 s) of quiet** at the start and the end, so the voice never feels cut off.

## Prompts used

The voiceover line:

```text
Every morning at Chai Corner, the kettle starts before the sun. Fresh ginger, real cardamom, and a smile in every glass.
```

The Style box:


## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: write a 15 second voiceover for your own shots. Make two takes with two different styles, and pick one.
2. Count the words in your script. At a normal pace, is it under about 35 words? If your style is slow, cut a few more.
3. Record the same line on your phone in a soft room, two takes, and compare it with the text to speech take.

What good looks like: every word is clear, the voice fits inside the shots with half a second of quiet at each end, and the style matches the mood of the picture.

## Common mistakes

- **Recording in an empty, echoey room.** Use a small room with curtains, a bed or clothes.
- **Phone right in front of your mouth.** Your breath hits it. Hold it a hand's width away, a little to the side.
- **Only one take.** Say each line twice and keep the best.
- **Skipping the Style box.** The plain voice is flat; a short style description makes it warm and paced.
- **Too many words.** A 15 second ad holds about 35 words at a normal pace, fewer for a slow voice.
- **Long talking on camera.** Lip sync is the hard part. Keep it to one or two lines.
