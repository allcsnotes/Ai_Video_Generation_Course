# M10-L04 · Sound Effects and Levels

> Video: Module 10, Lesson 4. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

The video opens with a mix where the music is as loud as the voice, so the words get lost. Then the fixed mix, with two small sound effects. This lesson shows how to add those effects, and how to set the levels so the voice always stays clear.

## What you'll learn

- What sound effects are, and why muted AI shots need a few back
- How to find free sound effects on Pixabay
- How to place each effect exactly on its action
- The three layers of a mix, and the 12 dB starting point
- How to check your mix the way people will really hear it

## Key ideas

### Small sounds, a real scene

**Sound effects** are the small sounds of a scene:

- tea being poured
- a glass on the counter
- a scooter going past

Our AI shots were muted (Module 10, Lesson 1), so those sounds were gone. Adding a few back makes the picture feel real.

| Mix | Effects layer |
|---|---|
| Without | Empty |
| With | The pour, then the glass |

You may not notice them. But you would miss them.

### Find them free on Pixabay

Pixabay has free sound effects too, under the same free license as its music (Lesson 3). Nothing here spends credits.

1. On Pixabay, click the **Sound Effects** tab.
2. Search for the **action, not the feeling**. The video typed `pouring tea`.
3. Play a few, and pick the one that matches your shot. The video picked **Tea Pouring**, and also took a glass being set down on a table.
4. Check for the **shield** here too (registered with Content ID), and keep the link as proof.

### Put each effect on its action

- Each effect must land on its action. Put the pour exactly where the tea starts pouring in the picture.
- Most files start with a little **silence**. Cut it off, or the sound will come late.
- Keep effects **short**: only the part you need, with a tiny fade at the end.

### The levels: three layers, loud to quiet

| Layer | Level |
|---|---|
| Voice | On top. Always the clearest sound |
| Sound effects | A little lower |
| Music and background | Well below the voice |

A **decibel (dB)** is the unit for how loud a sound is. A simple start: put the music about **12 dB below the voice**. If you cannot hear every word, turn the music down more.

When nobody is speaking, you can raise the music a little. Lowering the music under the voice, and bringing it back up when nobody speaks, is called **ducking**.

### Check it the way people hear it

1. Play it on your **phone speaker, at a low volume**.
2. Then play it again with **earphones**.
3. If any word is hard to catch, **lower the music**. Do not just push the voice up.
4. Do not make everything as loud as possible. YouTube turns very loud videos down anyway: you only lose the clean sound.

## Worked example

The Chai Corner mini ad: three muted shots (pour, close-up, wide), one street background, the warm voiceover, and the Santoor Sunrise music.

1. **The bad mix:** music as loud as the voice. Words get lost.
2. **Effects:** Pixabay > Sound Effects > `pouring tea` > Tea Pouring, plus a glass set down on a table. Silence cut from the start of each file.
3. **Placement:** the pour lands where the tea starts pouring; the glass lands on its touch.
4. **Levels:** voice on top, effects a little lower, music and background well below, starting at about 12 dB under the voice.
5. **Check:** phone speaker at low volume, then earphones. Every word is clear.

## Common mistakes

- **Music as loud as the voice.** The words get lost. Start about 12 dB lower.
- **Searching for a feeling** ("calm morning") instead of the action ("pouring tea").
- **Leaving the silence at the start of the file.** The sound comes late.
- **Long effects.** Use only the part you need, with a tiny fade.
- **Pushing the voice up** when a word is hard to hear. Lower the music instead.
- **Making everything as loud as possible.** YouTube turns it down anyway.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: add two sound effects and one music track to your clip, and mix them so every word is clear.
2. Find one effect for a pour and one for a glass on Pixabay. Check both for the shield.
3. Play your mix on your phone speaker at a low volume. Write down any word you could not catch, and fix it by lowering the music.
