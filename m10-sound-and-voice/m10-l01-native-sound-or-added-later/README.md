# M10-L01 · Native Sound or Added Later

> Video: Module 10, Lesson 1. Watch it first, then use this page to revise and practise.

The video opens with a clip that came with its own sound. Her voice, the street, and her moving lips all came from one prompt. That is **native sound**: sound the AI tool makes together with the picture.

## What you'll learn

- What native sound is, and what it gives you for free (lip sync)
- The catch: native sound is baked in, and every clip brings its own background
- The other way: mute the clips and build the sound yourself in the editor
- Where a background sound can come from (a free library, or one you make in Flow)
- How to decide, shot by shot, before you write the prompt

## Key ideas

### Native sound: made together, in one go

Omni (in Google Flow, Module 7) makes the picture and the sound together, in one go.

- Her lips move with her words.
- In the glass clip, the tap lands right when the glass touches the counter.

For a person speaking on camera, that is hard to beat. You get **lip sync** (lips matching the words) for free.

### The catch: native sound is baked in

The clip's sound is **one track**: the voice and the street noise come mixed together.

| You want to... | With native sound |
|---|---|
| Pull the voice out | Only by making the clip again |
| Fix one word | Only by making the clip again |

### Each clip brings its own background

The video cuts our three stall shots together, each with its own native sound. The street sound **jumps at every cut**: louder, then quieter. The ear notices, even when the eye does not.

### The other way: mute the clips, build the sound

Mute the clips, and add the sound yourself in the editor (CapCut comes in Module 11). One track per sound:

| Track | What goes on it |
|---|---|
| Video | The shots, muted |
| Background | One background sound under all the shots |
| Voice | The voiceover |
| Music | One music track |
| Effects | Small sounds like a pour or a glass tap |

### Where the background comes from

Two choices: a free sound library, or make one yourself.

The video asked Google Flow for an empty street at sunrise, with this sound: soft birds, a light breeze, and distant traffic. No music, no voices.

- Cost: **5 credits**.
- You only keep its sound, not its picture.

### The result: one real place

Same three shots, muted, with that one background underneath. Now the street sound flows from shot to shot, like one real place. And you can turn it up, turn it down, or swap it any time, without making a new clip.

### Which one? Ask what the shot needs

| The shot | What to do |
|---|---|
| Someone speaks on camera | Keep the native sound. Lip sync is the hard part, and Omni already did it |
| Products, scenery, a montage under a voiceover | Mute, and add the sound later |
| A mix of both | Keep the native voice, turn its background down, and run one music track across everything |

### Decide before you prompt

If you will add sound later, put this in the prompt, so nothing unwanted gets baked in:

```text
No music. No voices.
```

## Worked example

The Chai Corner stall, three shots.

1. **Native:** the three shots cut together with their own sound. The street noise jumps louder, then quieter, at each cut.
2. **Background bed:** one Flow clip of an empty street at sunrise (birds, breeze, distant traffic; no music, no voices), 5 credits. Only its sound is used.
3. **Added later:** the three shots muted, with that one background under all of them. The sound is steady from shot to shot.
4. The talking clip (from Module 7) keeps its native sound, because she speaks on camera and the lips already match.

## Common mistakes

- **Cutting native clips together and not listening.** The background jumps at every cut. Always play the cut with sound on.
- **Hoping to fix one word later.** Native sound cannot be pulled apart. Fix it by making the clip again, or plan to add the voice later.
- **Forgetting "No music. No voices." in the prompt.** Unwanted music or chatter gets baked in, and you cannot remove it.
- **Muting a talking clip.** You throw away free lip sync. Keep native sound for people speaking on camera.

## Try it yourself

1. The video's task: cut three of your clips together and listen. Then mute them, and lay one background under all three.
2. For each of these shots, decide native or added later: a close-up of a glass of chai; the chai seller saying a line to camera; a slow pan across the stall under a voiceover.
3. Write one prompt line you would add to a product shot that you plan to score later.
